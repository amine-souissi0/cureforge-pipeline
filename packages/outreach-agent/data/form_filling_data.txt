Please describe the scientific basis for your product. How does it work?


CureForge is a fully autonomous self grown ng multi‑agent AI system that performs the entire research cycle of a longevity institute – autonomously and continuously.


Core components:


·Global Director agent sets research priorities and allocates compute resources across disease domains (neurodegeneration, metabolism, immune aging, etc.).
·
Domain Supervisor agents manage specialized teams of Research Worker agents.


·Research Workers execute five tasks in a handoff pipeline:


verify trial data integrity,
improve study design,
mine PubMed/ClinicalTrials.gov for new evidence, generate cure hypotheses using a Longevity Knowledge Graph (genes, pathways, drugs, and the 12 Hallmarks of Aging),
propose novel experiments.


Causal gatekeeping: Before any hypothesis is simulated, a Causal Inference Engine (DoWhy with Pearl’s do‑calculus) performs refutation tests to reject spurious correlations preventing resource waste on false leads.


Digital twin validation: Promising interventions are tested on billions of virtual humans (molecular, cellular, tissue, and organism layers) using a PROCOVA‑compliant simulator that produces synthetic control arms accepted by the EMA and FDA.


End‑to‑end automation: A champion finding automatically triggers patent drafting, FDA IND/NDA package generation, grant proposal writing, and manuscript submission – closing the loop from discovery to regulatory filing without human intervention.


Thus, the system is not a tool for scientists; it is the scientist itself – a self‑improving, hierarchical AI institute with self evaluation and self protection engines

Does your product need regulatory approval (i.e., FDA)? If so, what is the approval process? What is your plan to get approved, and how much time and money will it take?


The CureForge platform itself is a software tool for research and simulation. It does not directly administer drugs to humans. Therefore, it does not require FDA approval as a medical device or therapeutic.


However: The outputs of the platform - champion hypotheses, digital twin trial designs, and IND/NDA packages – are intended to support regulatory submissions. The FDA’s Model‑Informed Drug Development (MIDD) program and the EMA’s qualification of digital twins (PROCOVA) already accept simulation evidence as part of IND/CTA packages. Our system is built to comply with those standards.


Plan for regulatory alignment (not approval of the platform itself):


Engage FDA via MIDD Paired Meeting Program within 12 months to pre‑qualify our digital twin simulator.


Maintain compliance with 21 CFR Part 11 (electronic records) for audit trails.


Work with first pilot partners to submit a real IND using our auto‑generated packages, establishing precedent.


Time & money: MIDD meeting request - 6 months; preparing the submission package - 3 months; no direct FDA fees for software tool. Legal/regulatory consulting: ~$50K.
