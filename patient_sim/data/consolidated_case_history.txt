SECTION 7. RECOMMENDATIONS FOR NURSES (CARE)
7.1 Right arm positioning
Pillow under the whole arm — not dangling. Shoulder slightly raised.
Arm abducted 30-45° from the body, elbow slightly flexed.
Palm up, fingers open (roll in the hand), thumb abducted — do NOT let it clench into a fist.
Do NOT pull on the paralyzed arm when turning (shoulder dislocation risk).
7.2 Right leg positioning
Pillow under the shin. HEEL floating free (not on mattress) — pressure-sore prevention.
Knee slightly flexed (~10-15°). Foot at 90° to the shin (support) — foot-drop prevention.
Roll beside the hip — against outward rotation.
Left foot stump (post-amputation) — no direct pressure, inspect at every turn.
7.3 Turns and pressure sores
Reposition every 2 hours: back -> left (healthy) side -> back. Do NOT place on the right (affected) side.
Risk zones: sacrum, heels (especially right), shoulder blades, elbows, occiput, ear, foot stump.
Sheet without wrinkles. Skin dry. Do NOT rub reddened areas.
7.4 Night warning signs (call doctor immediately)
Worsening consciousness, new facial asymmetry, sharp BP rise >160.
Noisy/gurgling breathing (aspiration risk), seizures, vomiting.
Bleeding from nose/gums (on anticoagulation), temperature >38°C.
Redness/pain at the neck surgery site.
SECTION 8. RECOMMENDATIONS FOR THE SPEECH THERAPIST
Leading deficit — motor (Broca's) aphasia with preserved comprehension. Multilingual patient: Russian (native), English, Swahili.
8.1 Evidence-based methods
Melodic Intonation Therapy (MIT) — singing/intoning phrases; proven for motor aphasia with preserved comprehension.
Semantic Feature Analysis (SFA) — naming via object features.
Script Training — 5-10 functional phrases to automaticity ('I need water', 'Where is my granddaughter?').
Intensity >=3 hours/week, short frequent sessions.
8.2 Multilingual strategy
8.3 How to communicate with the patient
Short closed questions ('Want water?'), picture cards, time to respond.
Speak normally, as to an adult — he understands everything.
Do NOT demand 'say it properly', do not repeat questions many times.
Language mixing is a normal compensatory strategy, not an error.
SECTION 9. REHABILITATION AND CENTER CHOICE
9.1 Early rehabilitation principles
Short frequent sessions (2-3/day, 10-20 min), NOT an intensive marathon (per AVERT III, early high-intensity mobilization is harmful).
Mirror therapy for the right arm from the first days.
Nutrition: 1.2-1.5 g protein per kg body weight per day.
Sleep in darkness — important for neuroplasticity.
