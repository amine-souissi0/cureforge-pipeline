OVERNIGHT CARE
Body Positioning and Complication Prevention
Period: 22:00 — 08:00
Patient: Oleg I. T. | Post-stroke | Right-sided hemiparesis
SECTION 1. RIGHT ARM — CORRECT POSITIONING
Correct arm position -- step by step
What NOT to do with the arm
X  Do not leave the arm dangling off the bed -- even for 10 minutes. Shoulder subluxation can develop.
X  Do not pull on the paralyzed arm when turning the patient -- the ligaments are weakened and it can dislocate.
X  Do not let the hand clench into a fist for long periods -- a contracture forms.
X  Do not place the arm under the head or under the torso -- this compresses blood flow.
SECTION 2. RIGHT LEG — CORRECT POSITIONING
Correct leg position -- step by step
Special attention -- left foot after toe amputation
The patient has had toes 1-5 of the left foot amputated. This requires extra care for the left foot specifically:
The left foot stump must not press against the footboard or side rail
Place a soft pillow under the stump to avoid direct pressure
The stump skin is especially vulnerable to pressure sores -- inspect at every repositioning
Keep it warm (not hot) -- must not touch heating pads or warmed surfaces (reduced sensation, burn risk)
What NOT to do with the leg
X  Do not rest the heel on the mattress -- a pressure sore can form overnight.
X  Do not let the foot droop downward -- foot drop develops quickly.
X  Do not let the leg roll outward -- secure with a roll.
X  Do not use tight bandages or restraints without nursing approval.
SECTION 3. REPOSITIONING SCHEDULE
Reposition the body every 2 hours -- the golden rule of pressure-sore prevention. The 22:00-08:00 period = 5 turns.
If the nurse performs the turns -- what to check
Head position -- not unnaturally turned, chin not pressed into the chest
Right arm -- on a pillow, palm up, fingers relaxed
Right leg -- heel free, foot at a right angle to the shin
Sheet under the patient -- no wrinkles (any wrinkle = pressure-sore risk)
Skin dry -- with sweating the skin becomes vulnerable; ask for a sheet change
The 'left-side-lying' position -- in detail
SECTION 4. PRESSURE-SORE PREVENTION
Overnight risk zones are where bony prominences press against the mattress. In a bedridden patient 80+, a pressure sore can develop within 2-6 hours of pressure.
Risk zones (check at every turn)
Signs of a developing pressure sore
X  Skin redness that does not fade within 30 minutes after pressure is removed
X  A warm or hot area on palpation
X  Hardening of the skin or subcutaneous tissue
X  Tenderness on touch (if the patient can report it)
X  Color change (purple, bluish) -- this is already Stage 2, call the doctor urgently
Additional measures
Inspect the skin under risk zones at every turn -- with a flashlight, without pressing
Do not rub reddened areas -- this destroys small vessels and accelerates the sore
Keep skin dry but not over-dried -- moisturizer on non-reddened areas
With excess sweating, ask for a sheet change or place a cotton pad

These signs require immediately calling the nurse or on-call doctor -- do not wait until morning.
SECTION 6. ENVIRONMENT — THE OTHER HALF OF CARE
Light
Sleep in darkness is critical for neuroplasticity and recovery. Melatonin (the sleep hormone) is a powerful anti-inflammatory agent that lowers CRP. Full darkness is impossible in an ICU, but light exposure can be reduced.
+  Ask the nurse to dim the general lights in the room after 22:00 if possible
+  Bedside lamp -- point it away from the patient's face
+  Sleep mask -- if the patient tolerates it on the face, it significantly improves sleep quality
X  Do not shine a flashlight into the eyes when checking status -- illuminate only the needed area
Sound
+  Ventilators, monitors -- nurses can adjust alarm volumes without disabling them
+  Earplugs -- if the patient tolerates them. An ICU is a noisy place, and fragmented sleep harms recovery
X  Do not hold loud conversations near the patient -- even if he appears to be 'asleep'
Temperature
Optimal 20-22C. A patient with paresis may not feel the paralyzed side getting cold.
Light blanket, not heavy -- so it does not press on the legs and feet
Extra throw over the right leg -- it may cool down more than the left
Do not use electric heating pads on the paralyzed side -- reduced sensation, burn risk
SECTION 7. WHAT YOU CAN DO BEFORE LEAVING THE ROOM AT 22:00
If you have a chance to be with him before you leave -- a few practical things:
Check the right arm position -- is it on a pillow, palm up, fingers open
Check the right leg position -- heel off the mattress, foot at a right angle
Tell him calmly -- 'Dad, I'm leaving, I'll be back in the morning. You are safe, the doctors are nearby.' With Broca's aphasia he understands speech, and it matters to him
Hold his healthy (left) hand -- physical contact lowers stress and cortisol
If he reacts emotionally -- don't tell him 'don't be nervous.' Let him feel heard. 'I understand it's hard. I'm with you.'
Note on your phone the time of the last turn, temperature, BP -- for the morning handover
Agree with the nurse on a communication channel -- how they will inform you if something changes overnight
This guide is based on standards of nursing care for neurological patients after stroke. Most of these actions are performed by the nurse -- but knowing the standards helps you monitor care quality and raise specific questions. This is not a medical opinion.
